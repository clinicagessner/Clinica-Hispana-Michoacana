Women's health is not one visit a year: it is three or four things worth checking at each stage, plus a handful of warning signs that should not wait. This guide sorts both, so you know what is due now and what a walk-in clinic can handle.

## What to check by age

**Ages 20 to 39.** Pap smear from age 21, every three years. Blood pressure every year. Sexually transmitted infection testing if you are sexually active; the [CDC recommends](https://www.cdc.gov/sti/hcp/clinical-guidance/index.html) chlamydia and gonorrhea screening yearly before 25. And iron, if your periods are heavy: anemia is the most common cause of the fatigue people blame on stress.

**Ages 40 to 49.** The mammogram conversation begins. The [USPSTF recommends starting at 40](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/breast-cancer-screening), every two years. It is also worth checking blood sugar and cholesterol, especially if you had gestational diabetes.

**Age 50 and up.** Mammogram every two years, thyroid check if symptoms appear, and colon screening from age 45. Menopause brings changes in sleep, weight and mood that can be managed: there is no need to put up with them in silence.

## Signs that should not wait

- Bleeding between periods, after menopause, or far heavier than usual.
- Discharge with a bad smell, intense itching or burning when urinating that does not ease within a day.
- Pelvic pain that keeps increasing, with or without fever.
- A new lump in the breast, skin dimpling or nipple discharge.

None of those improve by waiting for the next appointment: they get checked when they happen.
