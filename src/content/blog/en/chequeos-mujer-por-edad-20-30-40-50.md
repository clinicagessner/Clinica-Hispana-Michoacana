At 25 hardly anyone thinks about cholesterol, and at 52 hardly anyone remembers what year their last Pap smear was. What is worth checking changes with every decade, because each stage brings its own to-do list: in your 20s family planning weighs more, in your 40s blood pressure and blood sugar start to creep in, and after 50 menopause and bone health take the stage.

This guide goes decade by decade. It is not a rigid calendar: based on your age, your history and your results, the medical team at Clínica Hispana Nueva Salud Michoacana tells you how often to repeat each item. Use it as a cheat sheet so you never walk into a visit empty-handed.

## The whole map at a glance

| What gets checked | Ages 20 to 29 | Ages 30 to 39 | Ages 40 to 49 | 50 and up |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pap smear | Starting at 21 | Yes, and HPV testing can be added | Yes | Yes, until you are told you can stop |
| Blood pressure | Every visit | Every visit | Every visit | Every visit |
| Blood sugar (glucose or A1C) | With a family history or extra weight | Same, and always after diabetes in pregnancy | Becomes routine | Routine |
| Cholesterol | A first baseline | Depending on your risk | More often | More often |
| Thyroid | If there are symptoms | With symptoms or after giving birth | If there are symptoms | Asked about more often |
| Birth control | Choosing a method | Checking it still fits | Still needed | Until menopause is confirmed |
| Bones | Calcium and exercise | Calcium and exercise | Habits that protect them | Risk gets assessed |

## In your 20s: getting off to a good start

Many women in this decade only come in when something hurts. Better one calm visit, with no emergency attached.

- **Pap smear starting at 21.** Our [gynecology](/en/services/ginecologia) page lays out the general schedule: from 21 to 29, every three years when results come back normal.
- **Blood pressure and weight.** High blood pressure at 24 does happen, especially when it runs in the family or with some hormonal birth control.
- **Birth control that fits you.** At the [birth control visit](/en/services/anticonceptivos) the pill and the shot are compared against your health and your routine. If you would rather have a long-acting method such as an IUD or an implant, we help you find where to get it placed, and once an implant has reached the end of its time, it can be removed here.
- **Periods that knock you out.** A very heavy period, one that lasts more than a week or leaves you drained, is not "just how the women in my family are": a blood test shows whether anemia has already set in.

Question to bring: "When is my next Pap smear due, and which method suits my health?"

## In your 30s: the checkup that keeps getting postponed

Between pregnancies, raising kids and double shifts, your 30s are the "I'll go later" decade:

- **Pap smear, with the HPV option.** From 30 on, the Pap test can be paired with HPV (human papillomavirus) testing, and then the interval can get longer. Your previous results decide which one applies to you.
- **Blood sugar if you had diabetes during pregnancy.** Gestational diabetes raises the chance of type 2 diabetes years later. The A1C, which needs no fasting, is a good way to keep an eye on it.
- **Your thyroid after having a baby.** Fatigue, hair loss or a low mood months after giving birth get blamed on sleepless nights, but sometimes the thyroid is behind them. Our [thyroid](/en/services/tiroides) page explains where it starts: with a TSH test, no fasting.
- **Rethinking the method you have always used.** If you smoke, get migraines with aura or your blood pressure has started to climb, some birth control options stop being a good idea.
- **A first lipid panel**, if you have never had one. This one does call for 8 to 12 hours without food.

Question to bring: "Given what happened in my pregnancies, what should I keep watching from now on?"

## In your 40s: when the numbers start to shift

You may feel just like you did at 30, but the lab work does not always agree. Blood pressure, sugar and cholesterol rise without symptoms, which is why this is the decade to measure instead of guessing.

- **A lab panel.** Glucose or A1C, a lipid panel and kidney function are run at the [clinic lab](/en/services/examenes-sangre). If a value comes back out of range, follow-up for [diabetes, blood pressure and cholesterol](/en/services/condiciones-cronicas) happens in the same place.
- **Periods change their rhythm.** Shorter, longer or heavier cycles are common as menopause approaches. Even so, bleeding between periods or bleeding that soaks pad after pad gets checked, and sometimes a pelvic ultrasound is ordered, which is done at the clinic.
- **Pregnancy is still possible.** With irregular periods fertility drops, but it does not vanish. Birth control is still needed until menopause is confirmed.
- **The mammogram conversation.** Mammograms are not done at the clinic: at the visit your history is reviewed and we help you find where to get one.

Question to bring: "Which of my numbers look fine, and which ones do I need to start looking after now?"

## At 50 and beyond: menopause, heart and bones

Menopause is confirmed after twelve months in a row without a period. Before and after that point, the body changes in ways you notice (hot flashes, night sweats, trouble sleeping, vaginal dryness, mood swings) and in ways you do not notice until something is measured.

- **Bleeding after menopause always gets checked.** Even if it is a little and only once.
- **The heart takes over the spotlight.** After menopause, the odds of high blood pressure and high cholesterol go up.
- **Hot flashes that keep you up at night.** They can be managed. At the visit your symptoms are reviewed and look-alike causes are ruled out, such as an overactive thyroid.
- **Bones.** With menopause, bones lose density faster. What protects them most is walking or weight-bearing exercise, eating enough calcium-rich foods and not smoking. Bone density scans are not done at the clinic; if your age or history calls for one, we help you find where to get it.
- **Colon.** Colon screening is generally recommended starting at 45. It is not done here either, but it belongs in the conversation.
- **Pap smear**, depending on your earlier results, until you are told you can stop.

Question to bring: "Is what I am feeling menopause, or is there something else worth checking?"

## What stays the same at any age

- **Vaccines.** The flu shot every year; the tetanus booster every ten years. Both are given here.

## Signs that do not wait for any checkup: call 911 or go to the ER

- Bleeding so heavy that it makes you dizzy or you pass out.
- Severe pelvic pain that comes on suddenly.
- You are pregnant or think you might be, and you have sharp one-sided pain or any bleeding.
- High fever together with pain low in the belly.

## What to bring so the visit pays off

More than knowing every test by name, bringing facts helps: the date of your last period, the rough year of your last Pap smear, any old lab results saved on your phone, the list of what you take every day, and which illnesses run in your family, especially diabetes, high blood pressure and breast or colon cancer.

If you leave the visit with a prescription, there is no extra stop to make: whatever the medical team prescribed is bought right there, at the clinic pharmacy.
