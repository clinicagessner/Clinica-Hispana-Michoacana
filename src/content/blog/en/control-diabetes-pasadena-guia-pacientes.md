Managing diabetes comes down to three things: knowing which numbers to watch, measuring them at the right intervals and adjusting when they drift. The rest is noise. This guide covers the numbers, the intervals and what actually moves them.

## The numbers that matter

**Hemoglobin A1C.** Your average blood sugar over the past two or three months. The [American Diabetes Association](https://diabetes.org/about-diabetes/a1c) puts the target for most adults below 7 %, though your clinician may set a different one based on your age and other conditions.

**Home glucose.** Fasting, the usual aim is 80 to 130 mg/dL, and two hours after eating, under 180.

**Blood pressure and cholesterol.** Diabetes does its damage mainly through the heart and kidneys, so blood sugar alone is not enough: blood pressure and LDL get watched just as closely.

## How often to check

A1C every three months while above target, twice a year once at goal. Blood pressure at every visit. Lipid panel and kidney function once a year. Foot check at every visit, and a dilated eye exam once a year. That last one always gets skipped, and it is what saves your vision.

## What actually moves the needle

- **A walk after meals.** Ten or fifteen minutes blunt the post-meal spike better than walking on an empty stomach.
- **The size of the rice, bread or tortilla portion.** You do not need to eliminate them; you need to measure them.
- **Drinks.** One large soda or juice can undo a whole day of effort. Water is the cheapest thing you can do for your A1C.
- **Taking medication at the same time daily.** Skipped doses show up in the result three months later.

## Warning signs

Constant thirst and urination, blurry vision, unintended weight loss, foot wounds that will not close, tingling in hands or feet, or low-sugar episodes with shaking and cold sweats. Any of those deserves a prompt visit, not a wait for the next check.
