A lot of people go years without having their heart checked because of one idea that keeps circulating in waiting rooms: "that's hospital stuff, and without insurance it will cost a fortune." The reality is usually simpler. A first heart check at a general clinic rests on three things that need no operating room and no hospital stay: taking your blood pressure properly, drawing some blood work and, when it makes sense, recording your heart's electrical tracing. What you do need is to know what to ask for and what to ask about before you pay, so every part of the visit is worth it.

## Start by saying why you came

Coming in "because it's about time" is not the same as coming in because something is bothering you. Before asking for tests, figure out which of these fits you:

- **You feel fine, but nobody has checked you in a long time.** Maybe you are past 40, you smoke or used to, you carry extra weight, or high blood pressure or heart trouble runs in your family.
- **You already have a number that worries you.** The machine at the grocery store showed high blood pressure, or an old lab result flagged high cholesterol and you never followed up.
- **You notice something new.** You get winded on the stairs more than before, your heart races or skips now and then, or you feel lightheaded when you stand up.

Saying it plainly, in your own words, helps the clinic's medical team decide what makes sense today and what can wait. When the money comes out of your own pocket, that filter is worth a lot.

## What you can ask for, layer by layer

At La Michoacana the heart is checked with what the clinic has under one roof: the visit itself, its own lab and an electrocardiogram. This table sums up what to ask for, what each one tells you and how to come prepared:

| What to ask for | What it tells you | How to come in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blood pressure reading at the visit | Whether your pressure is in range or needs watching | No coffee or cigarettes shortly before, and a few minutes seated |
| Lipid panel | The fats in your blood: good cholesterol, bad cholesterol and triglycerides | A fast of 8 to 12 hours, water allowed |
| Glucose and A1C | How your blood sugar is doing, which also strains the arteries | Glucose fasting; A1C needs no fast |
| Kidney function | How your kidneys are holding up, which goes hand in hand with blood pressure | Drawn from the same blood sample |
| Electrocardiogram (EKG) | Heart rhythm and signs of previous damage | No preparation; it takes about five minutes |

Blood pressure is the first and cheapest thing to measure, and still the one most often skipped. The [American Heart Association](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/high-blood-pressure/understanding-blood-pressure-readings) defines high blood pressure as starting at 130/80, and it rarely causes symptoms. A single reading is not enough to label it, which is why it is worth having it taken at the visit and compared with others.

The blood work is done at the [clinic's lab](/en/services/examenes-sangre). If you plan on a lipid panel, come in the morning so the fast doesn't drag on, and if you take daily medication, ask before skipping a dose.

For the [electrocardiogram](/en/services/electrocardiograma) you lie down for a few minutes with patches stuck to your chest, arms and legs. You feel nothing, because the machine sends no current: it only reads what your heart generates. You don't need an order to request it. Keep in mind that a normal EKG does not rule out everything: some arrhythmias come and go, and some blockages don't show at rest. That is why it is read together with your symptoms, your blood pressure and your lab work, not as a stamp that says "healthy heart."

## How to ask at the front desk

If you feel awkward not knowing the names of the tests, don't worry. These lines work as they are:

- "I'd like to get my heart checked. What do you suggest to start with?"
- "A pharmacy machine said my blood pressure was high. Can you take it again and tell me if I need lab work?"
- "I want to know where my cholesterol is. Do I have to come in fasting?"
- "My job wants an EKG. Can you do it without an order?"

## Questions that spare you surprises

### About what the visit includes

Ask whether the visit includes the blood pressure reading and a review of your results, or whether each test is separate. If a lab test or an EKG is suggested, it is fine to ask what it is for in your case and what would change depending on the result. A good answer sounds like "if it comes back high, we adjust this," not "just in case."

It is also worth asking who will explain the result. Here you receive the EKG tracing along with its interpretation, and lab results are explained in Spanish, so you don't walk out with a page full of abbreviations.

### About how you pay

Ask for the price before anything starts: the clinic gives it over the phone or when you arrive. Cash, debit cards, credit cards (Visa, Mastercard, American Express and Discover) and phone payments are accepted; checks are not. If the visit leads to a test you had not planned on, ask how much it adds before saying yes. You can say "just the blood pressure and cholesterol today, I'll do the EKG next time" if that works better for you, unless the medical team explains that it shouldn't wait.

### About follow-up

Getting checked once does little if nobody looks at the numbers again. Ask:

- **How often to come back.** For people already under care, blood pressure is checked at every visit and cholesterol is repeated once or twice a year depending on risk and whether there is treatment.
- **What to do if it reads high at home.** If you own a blood pressure cuff, ask which numbers mean you should come back before your next date.
- **Where to buy what gets prescribed.** The clinic has its own [pharmacy](/en/services/farmacia): if the medical team prescribes something, you buy it right there before you leave and get an explanation in Spanish on how to take it.
- **What happens with your current pills.** If you already take something for blood pressure or cholesterol, don't stop it or switch it on your own, even if you feel fine: talk to the medical team before changing anything.

If the numbers confirm high blood pressure, high cholesterol or out-of-range sugar, follow-up happens in the [diabetes, blood pressure and cholesterol care](/en/services/condiciones-cronicas) visit. And if something in the EKG or your symptoms calls for more testing, you get guidance on a specialized evaluation and the next step.

## Bring what you already have

Any earlier data saves repeating things. Gather on your phone:

- Photos of blood pressure readings from the pharmacy or your own cuff, with date and time.
- Previous lab results, even if they are from another country or several years old.
- The list of your medicines, including natural remedies and over-the-counter ones.
- Who in your family had a heart attack, a stroke or high blood pressure, and at what age.

## When it is not a clinic visit

A heart check is for when there is no emergency. Call 911 for intense chest pain or sudden trouble breathing, or if you notice one side of the face sagging, an arm with no strength or difficulty getting words out. Don't come to the clinic and don't drive yourself: the ambulance starts treating you as soon as it arrives.
